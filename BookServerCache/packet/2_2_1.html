2.2.1 Addressing Scheme

Rather than relying on callsigns explicitly, NET/ROM often uses node name aliases. For instance, your BBS could be namedNYBBS, and your own node could be named WECNOD. In order to build up a collection of nodes and adjacency, NET/ROM nodes emit node broadcasts on a fixed timer (which is inevitably changeable by the user); once adjacent nodes receive these packets, the virtual circuit tunnels open and NET/ROM packets may pass.

Some packet radio stacks (namely XRouter) refer to a NET/ROM connection as a "layer 4" connection; by a contrast, a no-L3 connection would be a "layer 2" connection. (10)
