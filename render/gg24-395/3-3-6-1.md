### 3\.3\.6\.1 SystemView Overview

<a id="HDRHSVW101"></a>

The SystemView strategy is a structure that enables the development of consistent systems management applications\. The framework will continue to evolve as conforming products are introduced\. The objective of the SystemView framework is to address client requirements for improved productivity of personnel involved in managing enterprise\-wide systems, increase systems and network availability, and deliver a consistent set of services to the end users\. The resources may be managed across Open Systems Interconnetion \(OSI\), TCP/IP, LANs, IBM's Systems Network Architecture \(SNA\), or Advanced Peer\-to\-Peer Networking \(APPN\)\. The SystemView structure is divided logically into four areas called dimensions:

**End\-use dimension**

The end\-use dimension describes the facilities and guidelines for providing a consistent user interface to the management system\. It provides the same look and feel for all management disciplines\. The end user interface will be based on the programmable workstation using Presentation Manager for OS/2 \(SAA\), or OSF/MOTIF for AIX/6000 \(AIX\)\. The user interface will be object\-oriented by using icons\. The primary benefit of the end\-use dimension is end\-user productivity\.

**Application dimension**

The application dimension consists of the SystemView applications, applications services, and the common management interface to agents\. Agents provide the functions necessary to interact with the applications that manage them\. The primary benefits of the application dimension are the automation of systems management, integration of functions, and implementation of open standards\.

**Data dimension**

The data dimension describes and supports the data needs of the system and network\. It allows sharing of systems management data to the highest degree possible\. The primary benefit of the data dimension is an improved base for quality management information\.

**Managed resource dimension**

This area of the SystemView framework deals with products that help manage the resources within the enterprise\.

Within these dimensions, there are six disciplines to which all systems management tasks belong:

**Business Management** The business management discipline includes tasks that support a wide range of business and administrative functions to run business aspects of an enterprise\-wide information system\. The tasks included are security management, inventory/asset control, billing and charge back, and budget planning\.

**Change Management** The change management discipline includes tasks that manage and control the introduction of change into a systems environment\. The tasks include planning, testing, and distribution of changes to data processing\.

**Configuration Management** The configuration management discipline is the collection of facilities and processes needed to plan, develop, and maintain the operational properties and interrelationships of resources within the enterprise's information systems\. The tasks include design, and updating configuration information\.

**Operations Management** The operations management discipline deals with tasks that plan, distribute, evaluate, and control workloads\. The tasks include workload and operations planning, management, and control\.

**Performance Management** The performance discipline addresses the effectiveness with which information systems deliver services to their customers\. The tasks include capacity, service planning, and control\.

**Problem Management** The problem management discipline is the process of managing problems, incidents, and critical situations from their detection through their final resolution\. The tasks include incident detection, recognition, and problem analysis and diagnosis\. The SystemView strategy addresses the information systems industry trends in distributed Client/Server computing and Open Systems\. This enterprise\-wide management strategy consists of the SystemView structure and conforming products\. The structure provides a consistent graphical end\-user interface, shared systems management data, enhanced automation, and increased integration among systems management products\. The SystemView infrastructure is designed to provide:

- End\-to\-end management capability
- Improved staff productivity
- Higher availability and continuous operations
- Integrated applications
- Cost reduction and control
- Return on investment
- Automation of processes and tasks
- Competitive and strategic business advantages
