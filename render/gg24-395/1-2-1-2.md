### 1\.2\.1\.2 VM Client/Server Redefined

In the 1980's, research at universities triggered a change in the technology and the information processing environment\. The programmable workstation allowed end users to address their needs on their own platforms\. Tools to solve these needs spread with the intensified use of workstations, creating a demand for more\. The focus went from centralized data processing to qualified information processing on distributed resources\. Although VM inherently allowed Client/Server scenarios, it lost its reputation as being an open Client/Server platform when the interest turned to the workstation\.

With the change to workstation computing came new hardware, software, and standards \(open\) that play a major role in defining Client/Server computing today\.

The VM Client/Server\-like implementations represent the "classical" scenario of someone \(a client\), requesting services from a resource \(server\) who provides them the service\. So when the term "Client/Server" was invented, it was not new to VM\. What this term and the environment related to it by the new definition introduces, is the emphasis on portability and interoperability with the non\-IBM and especially the UNIX world\. Remember that open standards enable systems to interact, and that is what Client/Server computing is about\.
