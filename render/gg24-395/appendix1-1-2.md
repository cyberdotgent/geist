## APPENDIX1\.1\.2 Standards Organizations

Listed in alphabetical order, are several major standards organizations\.

- Association Francaise de Normalisation \- AFNOR

AFNOR the French national standards body\. It participates in many of the ISO committees and subcommittees\. AFNOR is similar to ANSI in the United States and is one of the leading certification organizations in Europe\.

- American National Standard Institute \- ANSI

ANSI is a voluntary national standards coordinating body\. It approves standards and accredits groups that develop standards\. Most notably are ASCII, C, COBOL, and Fortran\. ANSI represents the U\.S\. in the International Organization for Standardization \(ISO\)\.

- British Standard Institute \- BSI

BSI approves and develops standards for the United Kingdom\. It participates in many of the ISO committees and subcommittees\.

- Comitee Consultatif International Telegrafique et Telephonique \- CCITT

CCITT is a voluntary worldwide group of public telephone and telegraph companies and regulated telephone companies\. CCITT is a permanent subcommittee of an agency of the United Nations, responsible for standardizing telecommunications\. CCITT's recommendations include, among others:

X\.25 X\.400 X\.500 ISDN \(Integrated Services Digital Network\)

Many parties, including IBM, contribute to CCITT\.

- Corporation for Open Systems \- COS

COS is a consortium affiliated with the ISO\. It was set up to provide independent conformance testing for OSI products and to accelerate the implementation of OSI related products\.

- Canadian Standard Association \- CSA

CSA approves and develops standards for Canada\.

- Deutsches Institut für Normung e\.V\. \- DIN

DIN approves and develops standards for Germany\.

- European Computer Manufacturers Association \- ECMA

ECMA is a non\-commercial, non\-profit organization open to vendors who develop, manufacture, or market information technology hardware or software products or services in Europe\. Its goal is to develop information technology and telecommunication standards for submission to international and European standards bodies \(ISO and IEC\)\.

- Institute of Electrical and Electronic Engineers \- IEEE

The IEEE is a voluntary standards organization accredited by the American National Standards Institute\. The IEEE has been responsible for the development of the Portable Operating System for Computer Environment \(POSIX\)\.

- International Organization for Standardization \- ISO

ISO is the international body responsible for adapting standards in many fields, including information technology\. Membership in ISO is limited to country standards organizations\.

- Japanese Industrial Standards Committee \- JISC

JICS approves and develops standards for Japan\.

- National Institute of Standards and Technology \- NIST

NIST is an agency of the U\.S\. Department of Commerce\. It sets and approves standards for the U\.S\. government procurement of information technology\. These standards are called FIPS \(Federal Information Processing Standards\)\. NIST also publishes profiles such as GOSIP \(Government OSI Profile\)\.

- Object Management Group \- OMG

OMG is a consortium of vendors including Hewlett\-Packard, Digital Equipment Corporation, SUN Microsystems, Microsoft, and IBM\. Its purpose is to promote the portability, reusability, and interoperability of software by producing a framework for object\-oriented environments\.

- Open Software Foundation \- OSF

OSF is a non\-profit consortium that was formed to do research and development on Open Systems that provide vendor independence\. Its aim is to define standards for a portable environment\. These standards are based on:

Relevant industry standards\. An open process involving all interested parties in the decision making process\. A timely, vendor\-neutral decision making process\. Provision of equal, early access to specifications and developments\. Provision of a hardware independent implementation\. Reasonable and stable licensing terms\. Technical innovation through research\.

The process used by OSF to develop standards is to issue Requests For Technology \(RFTs\)\. These are essentially invitations for suppliers \(not just OSF members\) to provide information on needed standards\.

- System Performance Evaluation Cooperation \- SPEC

SPEC is a consortium of workstation vendors formed to develop performance test methods\. A "SPECmark" is the published performance rating for systems benchmarked by the SPEC test site\. IBM is a member\.

- Unicode Consortium

The Unicode Consortium is a non\-profit organization founded to promote the use of Unicode as a standard\. The Unicode standard is used to encode written characters for storage and transmission\.

- UniForum

UniForum is an international organization of UNIX users, similar to SHARE and GUIDE for IBM system users\. UniForum was responsible for initiating work on the POSIX standards that has now been taken over by the IEEE\.

- UNIX International \- UI

UI is a group of vendors whose technology is based on ATC System V\.4 Interface Definition \(SVID\), and X/Open's Common Application Environment \(CAE\)\. The group was formed to counteract the influence of OSF, and to direct the development of UNIX systems\. Although consulted by AT&amp;T about future development, its role is to support, rather than contribute to, future versions of UNIX based on System V\.4\.

- X/Open

X/Open is a joint venture, rather than a standard body\. Originally, it was formed by a group of vendors in order to define a common foundation for the development of portable applications\. X/Open is geared towards commercial users, focusing more on product needs than on the wider issue of standards\.

X/Open appears to work much faster than the traditional standard organizations because it tends to utilize standards already adopted by other groups\. It selects standards and develops tests to ensure that compliant systems conform\.

X/Open does not deliver code\. The X/Open Portability Guide \(XPG\) is, however, the most complete consensus\-based portability specification in the industry\.
