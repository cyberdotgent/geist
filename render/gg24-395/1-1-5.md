## 1\.1\.5 Is Downsizing Rightsizing

<a id="HDRHWID100"></a>

As workstations and servers become more powerful and the applications they run become more robust, more businesses see them as a viable alternative to mainframe processing\. Downsizing, generally, relates to the moving of computing activities from large computers \- mainframes \- to one or more smaller computing systems\. Downsizing's greatest advantage is to give the users access to and control of the data\. Downsizing is not "business as usual" on a PC instead of a mainframe\. It involves a major change in the way the information is collected, processed, distributed, and managed\.

Downsizing does not necessarily mean removing dependence on mainframes altogether, but the essence of downsizing is to move day\-to\-day processing to a platform with a lower purchase price\. Existing mainframes can be the data storage and batch processing facility of the enterprise, pooling all the company\-sensitive or corporate\-wide databases, and making them available to all departments\. Acting as such, mainframes could exercise their known strengths of electronic and physical security of data, fast access to that data, and a centrally located backup\. There are some issues with downsizing\. Security is more difficult to maintain with multiplatform computing\. I/S typically is not involved in the details of the workstations and cannot assist with what they are not involved in\. Mainframe applications have to be recoded\. Support may come from several vendors, whose lack of knowledge of the mix of software would hinder concise problem solving\.
