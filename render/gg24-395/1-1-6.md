## 1\.1\.6 Client/Server Conclusions

We have described some of the characteristics of Client/Server applications\. It is apparent that the existence of distributed systems must be accepted as being a frequent scenario in new Client/Server implementations\. The systems chosen will, in all likelihood, not come from one vendor\.

Many consulting firms, including IBM, recognize the important decisions to be made in designing applications using Client/Server concepts, and have established offerings to help businesses integrate their system platforms for better efficiency\.

The creators of every hardware platform and software product must ensure that, if they are to remain competitive in the marketplace, their products will operate in a computing environment that is inextricably Open \- and IBM's VM/ESA is no exception\.

Developing Client/Server applications becomes an exercise in weaving a complex array of hardware and software alternatives into a working information system\. VM/ESA is just one of those systems that will play a part in the open world\.

Before discussing the new open world definitions, we'll have a look back at VM and Systems Application Architecture \(SAA\), comparing them to older definitions of both Client/Server and Open\.
