### 2\.5\.1\.3 Integrated Infrastructure

<a id="HDRHOOW202"></a>

There are many paths that can be used to provide open solutions for our customers\. Products can be built or migrated to standards; functions can be licensed from other sources and incorporated in IBM products; IBM technology can be offered to the standards community, or licensed to other vendors\. IBM will pursue all these avenues to provide our customers with the most effective solution to their requirements\. These approaches include:

- Frameworks

With the objective of supporting the customer's requirement for freedom of choice and systems management, IBM has defined and embraced the concept of "frameworks" for key technology or application areas\. Using frameworks, the overall definition of a system area, or application area, such as multivendor systems management, is defined and then broken into several manageable segments\. Interface definitions between the segments or tasks are then defined to allow the segments to work together\. These definitions are made generally available to the marketplace and software providers\. Under this concept, technology from several sources can be brought together to cohesively address a major business or system objective\. The Open Blueprint, one of the most important frameworks announced by IBM, forms the basis for IBM's view of Open Systems \(see [Chapter 5,](2-3.md) ["The Open Blueprint" in topic 2\.3](2-3.md)\)\. Other key frameworks that support the open environment include: SystemView Networking Blueprint AD/Cycle Information Warehouse

- Architectures Systems Application Architecture \(SAA\) was a significant step towards defining an architecture for open, distributed computing\. With the growth of UNIX\-based systems, it became clear that something more was needed\. With the introduction of OpenEdition MVS and VM, DCE and POSIX have been added along with SAA to provide the necessary portability and interoperability of applications that customers need\.

It is fully recognized that as the industry continues to evolve the requirements for Open Systems, customers will be faced with the difficult task of designing systems from multiple standards and protocols\. One of the intentions of the Open Blueprint was to make that decision easier for customers\.

As the frameworks and architectures continue to evolve, IBM will continue to work closely with national and international standards bodies to help define the protocols and interfaces that should be incorporated within these frameworks\.

- Standards\-based products

Utilizing the environments defined by the Open Blueprint, product solutions will be developed to address the specific requirements stated in [Part 2, "Overview of the Open World" in topic 2\.0](<#HDRHPRT200>)\. These products will allow customers to build an infrastructure that meets today's business requirements while not restricting them to a vendor\-specific platform\. The development of standards\-based offerings is the key to the development of truly open systems\.
