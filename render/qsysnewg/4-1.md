## 4.1 Assistance Levels

The AS/400 system allows you to choose the amount of assistance you want | to receive in your interaction with the computer. The various degrees of

assistance offered are referred to a**s assistan**c**e leve**ls. Assistance levels differ from one another in the following ways: | Terminology used | Types of tasks that can be performed | The number of functions and options supported that are actually shown | on the display | By now, you've probably guessed that the real difference between these | assistance levels is in how the computer communicates with you. The difference between assistance levels is in the number and complexity of the displays that you see. When you select a basic assistance level, the system simplifies your work by reducing the number of choices you need to make in performing your tasks. You can relax a bit, and let the computer do all the hard stuff!

On the other hand, when you select a more advanced assistance level, you | accept more of the responsibility involved in performing your tasks. You

may be wondering why in the world anyone would want *less* assistance from the computer. Well, having the ability to choose from a greater variety of options and functions can offer greater flexibility when performing some of the more complex tasks. Of course, when performing very basic tasks, there are no real advantages to having this additional flexibility.
