### 7.1.1 ISM Bands

*ISM is*

short for Industrial, Scientific, and Medical; these are radio bands intended for various devices of the same name. They are not intended for radio communication, but can be provided that the power is

[under a certain threshold. (63)](7-1-1.md)

For reference, here are most of the ISM bands in the USA:

<a id="TBLTBLUNIQ78"></a>

Table 7. US ISM Bands

| Start | End | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 13.553 MHz | 13.567 MHz | 22-meter band, some<br>run beacons here |
| 26.957 MHz | 27.283 MHz | CB radio |
| 40.66 MHz | 40.7 MHz | 6-meter band, an<br>amateur band in some<br>places that aren't<br>the US |
| 433.05 MHz | 434.79 MHz | Region 1<br>70-centimeter ISM<br>band<br>shared with an<br>amateur band |
| 902 MHz | 928 MHz | 33-centimeter band,<br>an amateur band in<br>the US |
| 2.4 GHz | 2.5 GHz | 13-centimeter band,<br>commonly used for<br>Wi-Fi and microwave<br>ovens |
| 5.725 GHz | 5.875 GHz | 5-centimeter band,<br>commonly used for<br>newer Wi-Fi systems |


There are some other bands, but they have been omitted for brevity.

<a id="FTNFTNUNIQ77"></a>

*! (63) Note that this guide is very USA-centric, and this rule may* *not* *apply* in some other countries!.
