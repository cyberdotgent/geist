MVS used to require that business objectives be translated into extremely technical terms in order for MVS to execute them. To specify the service objectives, MVS supplied hundreds of initialization and tuning parameters. The MVS system programmer had to allocate and adjust the amount of computer resources assigned to each user, so that all Service Level Agreements could be reached by manipulating the initialization and tuning parameters. This process can be protracted and error prone and may result in conflict with the original business objectives. The new MVS WLM introduces a *goal-oriented* management philosophy, allowing the user to explicitly state the system performance expectations, using business language like that used for the Service Level Agreements.
